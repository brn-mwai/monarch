\title{What does a released average-subject brain encoder actually predict? A noise
ceiling, a parcel-level check, and three silent failures}

\author[cuea]{Brian Mwai\corref{cor1}}
\ead{mwaibryn@gmail.com}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}

\begin{abstract}
\textit{Background:} Released brain-encoding checkpoints are increasingly used as instruments,
their predictions read as a proxy for cortical response. A release does not certify that the
checkpoint, in the configuration its loader selects, predicts held-out cortex. A
self-published audit reported that the released TRIBE~v2 checkpoint, in the average-subject
configuration its loader forces, is anti-correlated with cortex at vertex level
($r = -0.0145$ against an inter-subject ceiling of $+0.0508$).

\textit{New method:} A validation protocol on public Algonauts~2025 (CNeuroMod) data that runs
on CPU from a saved prediction: a leave-one-subject-out noise ceiling measured on the same
scans as the encoder, projection of predictions into 1000 Schaefer parcels, alignment of
prediction and recording on one clock in seconds, and a circular-shift null.

\textit{Results:} The ceiling for \emph{Friends} episode s01e01a is $0.1517$ (95\% CI
$[0.1484, 0.1551]$; four subjects, 592 timepoints). Over the first 120~s (80 scans) the
checkpoint gives a group mean $r = +0.0283$ against a same-scan ceiling of $0.0959$,
$p = 0.048$ against 61 circular shifts, with three of four subjects positive and the mean
positive for response delays of 3 to 7~s.

\textit{Comparison with existing methods:} Index-by-index comparison of a 2~Hz prediction with
a 1.49~s recording had produced a plausible, withdrawn $r = +0.0344$. Parcel-level values are
not directly comparable with the audit's vertex-level values.

\textit{Conclusions:} On this window the checkpoint is weakly positive and short of decisive.
The result does not reproduce the audit's negative sign; it does not refute it. Three silent
defects that changed reported numbers without an error are documented with safeguards.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A released encoding model invites a particular kind of use: treat the checkpoint as an
instrument, run it on stimuli its authors never considered, and read its predictions as a
stand-in for measured cortical response. The appeal is obvious, since the alternative is
scanning people. The risk is equally obvious once stated, which is that a technical report
describing an architecture is not a warranty about the weights released under its name, and
that a checkpoint may be released in a configuration whose predictive validity nobody has
established.

TRIBE, a trimodal encoder combining pretrained text, audio and video representations, won the
Algonauts 2025 challenge \cite{tribe2025}. Its successor, TRIBE~v2, is released as a
checkpoint with a loader that predicts responses on the fsaverage5 cortical surface
\cite{tribev2}. The loader in the public repository sets the model to its average-subject
configuration at load time, so a downstream user who follows the documented path receives a
single group-level prediction.

For this configuration the risk is not hypothetical. A commercial laboratory has published an
audit of the released checkpoint in its average-subject configuration \cite{sapient2026},
reporting vertex-level $r = -0.0145$ against a measured inter-subject ceiling of $+0.0508$:
anti-correlated with recorded cortex, across four subjects and all seven Yeo networks
\cite{yeo2011}. Taken at face value, that would mean downstream studies using this checkpoint
as an instrument read a signal with no established relationship to cortex.

The audit should not be accepted uncritically. It is self-published by a company selling the
per-subject alternative, it uses four subjects and one stimulus, and it reports magnitudes
near $0.015$ in either direction, so it is a claim about sign rather than about effect size.
Neither should it be dismissed. It is specific, it is falsifiable, and it concerns a
configuration that a released loader selects by default.

This paper asks the audit's question on public data, at parcel level, with a protocol that can
be rerun on CPU from a saved prediction. It reports a model-free noise ceiling, a prediction
pass over the first 120~s of one episode, and three silent defects met along the way, each of
which produced a plausible number without raising an error.

\section{Materials and methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Data}

We use the publicly released Algonauts 2025 responses \cite{algonauts2025}, recorded by the
Courtois NeuroMod project (CNeuroMod) \cite{cneuromod2023}: four subjects (sub-01, sub-02,
sub-03 and sub-05) watching the same episodes of the television series \emph{Friends}, sampled
every 1.49~s and distributed as time series for the 1000 parcels of the Schaefer atlas
\cite{schaefer2018} (7-network ordering) in MNI152NLin2009cAsym space. All analyses use
episode \texttt{s01e01a}. Each subject's recording is truncated to the shortest across
subjects, giving 592 timepoints.

\subsection{Noise ceiling}

An encoder's predictions are uninterpretable without knowing how much stimulus-driven signal
is shared across subjects. We estimate it with a leave-one-subject-out procedure: each
subject's parcel time series is correlated, over time and per parcel, with the mean of the
remaining three subjects. This is an inter-subject correlation \cite{nastase2019}, computed
from the recordings alone with no model involved. Because the mean of three subjects is itself
noisy, the estimate is conservative: a noiseless model of the shared response could exceed it
\cite{hsu2004,lagecastellanos2019}. It is used here as a reference scale, not as a hard upper
bound.

Undefined correlations stay undefined: a parcel with no variance over time has no Pearson
correlation, and substituting zero would pull the mean toward zero with invented values.
Parcel values are averaged within each subject, and the group value is the mean over
subjects. Confidence intervals are percentile bootstrap over subjects (10,000 resamples,
fixed seed), so a reported interval reproduces exactly.

\subsection{Checkpoint and prediction}

The released TRIBE~v2 checkpoint \cite{tribev2} is loaded through its own loader, which forces
the average-subject configuration. The checkpoint emits one prediction per 0.5~s of stimulus
(its feature rate is 2~Hz) for 20,484 fsaverage5 vertices. The encoder pass is bound by video
feature extraction, at about 17 minutes per 60~s of video on the GPU available (an NVIDIA
Tesla P100 in a free-tier session). A whole-episode pass exceeded the 12-hour session limit
before producing output, so the first 120~s of \texttt{s01e01a} were predicted, giving 241
prediction samples. The saved prediction is the input to every analysis below, all of which
run on CPU.

Predictions are projected from fsaverage5 vertices into the 1000 Schaefer parcels by averaging
the vertices carrying each parcel label in the surface version of the atlas
\cite{schaefer2018}; all 1000 parcels receive vertices. The prediction is projected down
rather than the recordings upsampled, which would invent within-parcel structure the data do
not have.

\subsection{Temporal alignment}
\label{sec:alignment}

The checkpoint was trained against BOLD shifted back by 5~s (the offset in its training
configuration), so the prediction for stimulus time $t$ estimates the scan recorded at
$t + 5$~s. For each recorded scan $j$, the stimulus time it responds to is $1.49j - 5$~s. The
prediction is linearly interpolated at that time for every scan whose stimulus time lies
inside the predicted window; scans outside it are dropped, not extrapolated. For the 120~s
window this leaves 80 scans, recording indices 4 to 83. The noise ceiling is then recomputed on
exactly these scans, so encoder and ceiling share one denominator.

The 5~s delay was taken from the checkpoint's training configuration and fixed before any
correlation was computed. Delays of 3, 4, 6 and 7~s are reported as sensitivity and are not
selected from.

\subsection{Statistical inference}

The encoder score for each subject is the mean over parcels of the Pearson correlation, over
time, between the aligned prediction and that subject's recording. The sign is reported as
measured; nothing is flipped or absolute-valued.

A bootstrap over four subjects has only 35 distinct resamples, so the encoder's bootstrap
interval is reported but not relied on. The test is a circular-shift null
\cite{nastase2019}: the aligned prediction is rotated against the recordings by every shift
from 10 to 70 scans (61 shifts), which preserves each series' autocorrelation and breaks only
their alignment, and the group mean correlation is recomputed at each shift. The one-sided
$p$ is $(b + 1)/(m + 1)$, where $b$ is the number of the $m$ shifts reaching the observed value
\cite{phipson2010}.

\subsection{Software}

Analyses use Python with NumPy and h5py. The noise ceiling, prediction pass and null are
separate scripts (see Data and code availability); each episode is matched by name and each
array's orientation is asserted against the atlas size of 1000 parcels.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Noise ceiling on the full episode}

For \texttt{s01e01a}, over four subjects, 1000 of 1000 parcels defined and 592 timepoints,
\begin{equation}
r_{\text{ceiling}} = 0.1517, \qquad 95\%\ \text{CI}\ [0.1484,\ 0.1551].
\end{equation}
Per-subject values are $0.1553$, $0.1548$, $0.1497$ and $0.1470$.

This is larger than the audit's $+0.0508$, and the difference is not a disagreement. Averaging
vertices within a parcel cancels independent noise and generally raises correlations, and the
two ceilings were measured on different stimuli and data releases, so a parcel-level ceiling
here and a vertex-level ceiling there are not comparable numbers.

Three of four subjects are positive and one is indistinguishable from zero. Two of the 61
shifted alignments reach the observed value, giving $p = 0.048$, at the margin of the
conventional threshold. Across response delays of 3 to 7~s (80 or 81 scans) the group mean
stays positive, from $+0.0239$ to $+0.0377$, so the sign does not depend on the delay chosen.
The ceiling on these 80 scans is lower than the full-episode $0.1517$; it is measured on fewer
and earlier scans, and the two are never mixed.

\subsection{Three silent failures}
\label{sec:failures}

Three defects in this work changed a reported number without raising an error. Each was
identified the same way: replaying the original code path on the same inputs reproduces the
original number exactly, which establishes the cause rather than merely fitting it. The values
below are withdrawn and are reported only to document the failure.

\paragraph{The episode was selected by position} An earlier ceiling of $0.2247$, CI
$[0.2147, 0.2363]$, described as covering ``482 parcels'', is withdrawn. The analysis code
carried a placeholder for the stimulus identifier and fell back to the first key in the
response file, which holds a different episode (\texttt{s01e02a}) from the one under
prediction. Subjects also do not agree on which session an episode is stored under, so
positional selection is inconsistent across files as well.

\paragraph{The array was transposed} The correlation routine expects arrays shaped as
units by timepoints. The recordings are stored as timepoints by parcels, and the orientation
guard tested whether the first axis
was longer than the second. For a $(482, 1000)$ array that test cannot fire, so every
correlation ran across the parcel axis. The ``482 parcels'' in the withdrawn figure was the
timepoint count. Replaying the original path reproduces $0.2247$, its interval and the count of
482 exactly.

\paragraph{Two clocks were compared index by index} The first completed prediction pass
reported an encoder correlation of $+0.0344$, which is withdrawn. The analysis truncated the
241-sample prediction and the recording to the shorter length and correlated them sample by
sample, so prediction $k$, at stimulus time $0.5k$~s, was scored against scan $k$, recorded at
$1.49k$~s. By the last sample the two were about four minutes apart. Replaying that path on the
saved prediction reproduces $+0.0344$ and all four per-subject values exactly. A synthetic
check shows the size of the error: two series drawn from one signal, one sampled as the
prediction is and one as the recording is, correlate at $r > 0.999$ once aligned and at
$r = -0.03$ when truncated and compared index by index.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Relation to the audit}

On this window the released checkpoint is not anti-correlated with the recordings in parcel
space. That is the full extent of the claim. The result does not reproduce the negative sign
the audit reports, but it does not refute the audit either. The audit measured vertices on a
different stimulus; this work measured parcels on 120~s of one episode. Parcel averaging raises
correlations, so a positive parcel-level value is compatible with a vertex-level value near
zero, and $p = 0.048$ from a single window of 80 scans is weak evidence that one further
window could overturn. A strict replication requires vertex-level CNeuroMod derivatives and
the audit's stimulus.

\subsection{Comparison with existing methods}

The noise ceiling used here is the leave-one-out inter-subject correlation common in
naturalistic fMRI \cite{nastase2019}. It is conservative relative to estimators that correct
for noise in the reference, such as extrapolation from repeated responses \cite{hsu2004} or the
analytical and split-half ceilings of Lage-Castellanos et al. \cite{lagecastellanos2019},
which rely on repeated presentations or split data. The
circular-shift null follows established practice for autocorrelated time series
\cite{nastase2019}, with the $p$-value computed so that it cannot be zero \cite{phipson2010}.
The departure from common practice is the alignment step: equal array lengths are often
taken as evidence of equal sampling, and in this work that assumption alone produced a
plausible positive correlation from misaligned series.

\subsection{Recommendations}

All three defects are generic, so we state three practices. Select records by name, never by
position. Decide array orientation against a known constant such as the atlas size, never by
comparing axis lengths. Place prediction and recording on one clock, in seconds, before
correlating anything, and measure the ceiling on the same scans as the encoder.

\subsection{Limitations}

The prediction covers 120~s of one episode, 80 scans, and four subjects; the result is
marginal and may not hold over the full episode. Bootstrap intervals over four subjects,
including those on the ceilings, have few distinct resamples and understate uncertainty. The
prediction is projected into parcels through the surface version of the Schaefer atlas while
the recordings use its volumetric version in MNI space; the parcels correspond by label but
are not sampled identically. The 5~s response delay comes from the checkpoint's training
configuration and is a single shift rather than a fitted haemodynamic response. The
average-subject prediction is one time series compared against every subject, so it cannot
capture individual differences by design. Only the checkpoint's default configuration was
tested.

\subsection{Future work}

The immediate next step is the same aligned pass over the whole of \texttt{s01e01a} and
further held-out episodes, which requires GPU time beyond a single free-tier session.
Vertex-level comparison against the audit would require CNeuroMod surface derivatives.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The noise ceiling for the target episode is $0.1517$, CI $[0.1484, 0.1551]$, measured from
public data and independent of any model. The aligned prediction pass over the first 120~s
gives a group mean of $+0.0283$ against a ceiling of $0.0959$ on the same scans, at $p = 0.048$
against a circular-shift null: positive, weak, and short of decisive. It does not reproduce the
negative sign the audit reports, and at parcel level it is not directly comparable with the
audit's vertex-level $-0.0145$. Three withdrawn values and their causes are reported because
each was silent, each is reproducible by anyone attempting this measurement, and none would
have announced itself.
