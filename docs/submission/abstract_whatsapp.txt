*Measuring the External Field: A Cortical-Proxy Content Observable and the Mean-Field Bound on Media-Driven Opinion Change*

Brian Mwai (1050555), B.Sc. Physics
Supervisor: Dr. Songa Mutambi

*Abstract*
Media is routinely modelled in statistical physics as an external field acting on a population of coupled opinion states, and that field is almost never measured. This dissertation builds an instrument that measures a candidate observable from content itself, applies it to a corpus, and reports what the measurement can and cannot support.

Text is converted to speech and passed to TRIBE v2, a released encoder that predicts cortical response. Two networks are averaged from the prediction, affective salience and deliberative control, and the index is their signed difference. No participant was scanned.

Across 400 length-matched items in four categories, the categories separate (eta squared = 0.1068, p = 1.0 x 10^-9), carried by fear-activating content. A TF-IDF baseline classifies the same items far better (AUC 0.9758 against 0.6274), so the index is reported as a field observable, not a classifier. Each category comes from a single source dataset, so the separation cannot be attributed to framing rather than provenance. A second session replicates the result (ICC = 0.8725).

In the mean-field model media enters as a field h = alpha X. The coupling alpha cannot be identified from the corpus, so no value is quoted. What survives is a necessary condition, alpha >= h_c(beta J) / Delta X: the measured spread Delta X = 0.1241 gives alpha >= 4.29 at beta J = 2, a constraint on any future proposal of this mechanism.
