# Monarch demo, 90 seconds

Narration for ElevenLabs, with the visuals to cut against it. Numbers are spelled out because
text-to-speech reads digits inconsistently. No symbols, no abbreviations, no jargon.

Target pace is roughly one hundred and sixty words per minute, which puts the script at about
eighty-eight seconds. Read it once at that pace before generating; if it lands long, cut the
sentence marked OPTIONAL.

## Narration only, for pasting into ElevenLabs

Two articles can report the same event and leave you in completely different states. One
informs you. The other winds you up. You can feel the difference. Nobody can measure it.

So I built something that can.

Monarch takes a piece of writing, has it read aloud, and runs it through a released brain
model that predicts how a reader's cortex would respond. Out comes one number: whether the
emotional side or the reasoning side is predicted to lead.

Then I tested it properly. Four hundred articles, four groups, matched for length, all
scanned. The groups do come out different, by a margin the study was built to catch. But it
is driven almost entirely by fear-based writing. The outrage articles, the ones I expected to
stand out most, did not stand out at all.

I scanned three hundred and seventy five of them a second time, to check the tool repeats
itself. The group result held. Individual articles moved around too much to trust on their
own, so I do not claim any.

Here is where the physics comes in. Physicists model public opinion like a magnet flipping,
with media as the magnetic field. Nobody had ever measured that field. Now there is a number
for it, and a limit on how strong it would have to be to move a population.

Three papers, the code, and the working tool. All public.

## Notes

**OPTIONAL cut if long.** Drop "The outrage articles, the ones I expected to stand out most,
did not stand out at all." That saves about six seconds. It is the most interesting sentence
in the script, so cut something else first if you can.

**Voice.** Pick a measured, unhurried voice rather than an energetic one. The script's
credibility comes from stating limits out loud, and an excited read undercuts that.

**What the script deliberately does not say.** It never claims the tool detects manipulation,
never claims any brain was scanned, never mentions the amygdala, and never gives a verdict on
a single article. Each of those would be a claim the measurements do not support, and the
same rules apply here as in the thesis.

**One caveat worth keeping honest.** The line "nobody had ever measured that field" is fair
for content observables. Korbel and colleagues did measure a field for campaign spending,
which the papers cite. If the audience is physicists rather than general, change it to
"almost nobody had measured it, and never for the words themselves."
