# Figures: what goes in, what generates it

Every figure below is produced by a command in this repository. A figure that cannot be
regenerated does not go in a paper, and no figure is drawn from data that was not measured.

Screenshots are listed last and are **not** journal figures. Referees want vector graphics
from scripts; a screen capture of a user interface is evidence of process, and it belongs in
the thesis appendix and the software section.

**Output format.** The scripts currently write PNG at 200 dpi, which is fine for the thesis.
Journal submission needs PDF or EPS, so every figure script must gain a `--format` flag
before Paper 1 is submitted. Tracked as a to-do, not done yet.

## Paper 1: theory. No GPU, no scan, nothing measured

| # | Figure | Shows | Generated by | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | `F(m)` at three values of `βJ`, zero field | The single well splitting into two: the transition itself | `landau.py` | function exists, no figure script |
| F2 | Bifurcation `m*(βJ)` at `h = 0` | Where consensus becomes spontaneous, with `β = 1/2` fitted on the curve | `phase_boundary.py` | to build |
| F3 | Susceptibility `χ(βJ)` near `βJ = 1` | Divergence at the critical point, exponent `γ = 1` | `phase_boundary.py` | to build |
| F4 | `m` versus `h` at criticality, log-log | Exponent `δ = 3` as the slope | `phase_boundary.py` | to build |
| F5 | Phase diagram in `(βJ, h)` | Bistable region and the spinodal boundary `h_c(βJ)` | `phase_map.py` | computed, not drawn |
| F6 | `α_required = h_c(βJ) / ΔX` versus `βJ` | **The contribution.** How strong a content-to-opinion coupling must be before any media-driven transition is possible | `phase_boundary.py` | to build |
| F7 | Uniform field versus distributed field at matched mean | Whether heterogeneous media exposure suppresses or enables consensus | `rfim.py` | to build, ~2 weeks |

F2, F3 and F4 exist to prove the solver reproduces exact mean-field results before anything
is applied to data. A referee who cannot see that check will ask for it.

F6 is the paper's point. F7 is what lifts it above a textbook exercise, and it is the one
piece that needs the measured distribution rather than a single number.

## Paper 2: the measurement

Everything here waits on `corpus_naa.csv`.

| # | Figure | Shows | Generated by | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| G5 | Free-energy atlas at measured category means, swept over `α` | How the landscape would deform, with `α` swept and never fitted | `build_corpus_report.py` | built |
| G6 | `χ(NAA)` across the measured range | Where opinion is most sensitive to a change in media diet | `report_charts.py` | function exists |
| G7 | Measured `ΔNAA` against Paper 1's `α_required` curve | **The result.** Turns the null calibration into a numerical constraint | joins F6 | to build |

G7 is where the two papers join. It is the figure that makes a null publishable, because it
converts "we did not detect a coupling" into "the coupling would have to exceed this value,
and the measurement excludes that range".
