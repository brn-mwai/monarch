What we built. A pipeline that takes a piece of writing, has it read aloud, and
runs it through a released brain model to predict how a reader's cortex would
respond. It gives one number per article: whether the emotional side or the
reasoning side is predicted to lead. We then built a corpus of 400 articles in
four groups, matched for length, scanned all of them, and analysed the results.

What we found. The four groups do come out different, by a margin the study was
designed to detect. The difference is carried almost entirely by the fear-based
articles. The outrage ones, which I expected to stand out most, do not stand out
at all. And since each group came from a different source, I cannot separate the
writing style from the newspaper it came from. I say that plainly before the
result everywhere it appears.

I also rescanned 375 of the articles to check the tool repeats itself. The
group-level result held. Individual articles moved around too much to trust one
on its own, so I make no claim about any single article.

Two other things worth mentioning. I looked for Kenyan and African news data
with usable labels and could not find any, so the corpus is entirely American
and European, and I say so in the limitations. And I had to withdraw one of my
own numbers after finding the code behind it had picked the wrong stimulus and
flipped an array. I have written up the corrected version and the mistake.
