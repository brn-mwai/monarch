What we managed to build. A working pipeline that takes a piece of writing, has
it read aloud, and passes it through a released brain model to predict how a
reader's cortex would respond. From that it produces one number per article:
whether the emotional side or the reasoning side is predicted to lead. We then
built a corpus of 400 articles in four groups, matched for length, scanned all
400 on a GPU, and analysed the result. Everything is public and every number in
the thesis is produced by a script rather than typed in by hand.

What we found. The four groups do come out different, by a margin the study was
designed to detect. But the difference is carried almost entirely by one group,
the fear-based articles. The outrage articles, which I expected to stand out
most, do not stand out at all. And because each group came from a different
source, I cannot separate "this is the writing style" from "this is the
newspaper it came from". I say that plainly before the result everywhere it
appears, rather than hiding it at the back.

I also scanned 375 of the articles a second time to check the tool repeats
itself. The group-level result held up. Individual articles moved around too
much to trust one on its own, so I make no claim about any single article
anywhere in the thesis or on the site.

One thing I could not do. I looked for Kenyan and African news data with usable
labels and could not find any. Everything openly available and labelled is
American or European. So the corpus is English-language from international
sources, and I say in the limitations that the numbers describe that corpus and
not media in general. Building a labelled Kenyan set is the obvious next
project, and I would like to talk about it.

The four changes I need approved, in brief:

1. The brain model I use only sees the outer surface of the brain. The amygdala
   is deeper, so it is not in there. My proposal was built on it. I measure the
   balance between surface regions instead, and I never call it the amygdala.

2. The original formula divides by a number that turns out to be negative about
   half the time, which made it unusable. I subtract instead. This is the point
   you raised at the defence.

3. I could not pin down the constant linking my measure to the physics model.
   The estimate does not hold up on data it has not seen, so I report that it is
   not pinned down and quote no value for it.

4. The dataset my proposal names was withdrawn by its authors in 2024. I checked
   this directly. I substituted an openly licensed one.
