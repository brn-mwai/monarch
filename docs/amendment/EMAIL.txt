Subject: Thesis: amendment for your approval, and where the write-up stands

Dear Dr. Mutambi,

Attached is a four-page amendment to my proposal. I need your approval on it
before I finalise the thesis. The working tool is at https://monarch.brianmwai.com
if you would like to see what the project delivers.

Four things changed once I actually ran the model:

1. The released TRIBE v2 checkpoint only predicts activity on the outer surface
   of the brain, 20,484 points. The amygdala sits deep inside, so it is not
   included. My proposal's main measurement was built on it. I now measure the
   balance between surface regions instead, and I never call it the amygdala.

2. The original formula divides by a number that is negative about half the
   time, which made it unusable on real content. I subtract instead of dividing.
   This is the change you raised at the proposal defence, and running the model
   is what showed why it was necessary.

3. I tried to calibrate the number linking the brain measure to the physics
   model. On the partial runs it was indistinguishable from zero; on the
   complete corpus it no longer is, but the fit still performs worse than the
   sample mean on data it has not seen, and the estimate scales with a quantity
   this study does not measure. It is unidentified rather than zero, which is a
   weaker statement than either, and I quote no value for it anywhere.

4. The dataset my proposal names, NELA-GT-2021, was withdrawn by its authors in
   January 2024. I verified this against the Harvard Dataverse API with a valid
   account. I have substituted SemEval-2019 Task 4, which is openly licensed and
   labels the article rather than the publisher.

Where the work stands. The apparatus is built and tested and the 400-item corpus
is complete, verified row by row, and analysed. The categories separate at an
effect size the design was powered to detect, though the separation is carried
almost entirely by one category, and each category comes from a single source
dataset, so I cannot attribute it to framing rather than provenance. Section 3
of the attachment states that confound before the result.

Two things I did that were not in the plan, both because the result would not be
honest without them. A second scanning session over 375 of the items gives me a
test-retest measurement: the separation replicates, but individual item scores
are too noisy to call a single item, so the thesis makes category-level claims
only and gives no per-item verdict anywhere. And I withdrew one of my own
numbers, a noise ceiling I had reported earlier, after finding that the code
producing it had selected the wrong stimulus and transposed an array. Both
defects changed the value without raising an error. The corrected measurement
and the two failure modes are written up, since they are the kind of thing that
is easy for anyone repeating this to reproduce.

What I am writing. The dissertation is drafted at seven chapters with the front
matter, and compiles to a bound-ready document. Alongside it are three papers:

  Paper 1, the theory, complete at 12 pages. It states what any content
  observable must satisfy for media to drive a transition in a mean-field model,
  which can be checked before any data is collected. Intended for Physica A.

  Paper 2, the instrument and the corpus, complete at 10 pages. It supplies the
  measured quantity Paper 1 cannot, and reports the confound and the reliability
  openly. Intended for Physica A as the companion.

  Paper 3, on whether the released encoder predicts real cortex at all, drafted
  at 6 pages with one measurement still to run. Intended for Imaging
  Neuroscience.

I am close to done. What remains is the final prediction run for Paper 3, which
needs about an hour of GPU time that becomes available on 15 August, and your
approval on the amendment.

coupling in point 3 has no predictive value out of sample. Paper 3 is my attempt
to settle it.

Second, if you are able to endorse me for arXiv in physics.soc-ph, I can post
