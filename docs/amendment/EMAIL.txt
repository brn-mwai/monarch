Dear Dr. Mutambi,

Attached is a four-page amendment to my proposal. I need your approval on it
before I write the thesis. The working tool is at https://monarch.brianmwai.com
if you would like to see what the project delivers.

Four things changed once I actually ran the model:

1. The released TRIBE v2 checkpoint only predicts activity on the outer surface
   of the brain, 20,484 points. The amygdala sits deep inside, so it is not
   included. My proposal's main measurement was built on it. I now measure the
   balance between surface regions instead, and I never call it the amygdala.

2. The original formula divides by a number that is negative about half the
   time, which made it unusable on real content. I subtract instead of dividing.
   This is the change you raised at the proposal defence, and running the model
   is what showed why it was necessary.

3. I tried to calibrate the number linking the brain measure to the physics
   model. On the partial runs it was indistinguishable from zero; on the
   complete corpus it no longer is, but the fit still performs worse than the
   sample mean on data it has not seen, and the estimate scales with a quantity
   this study does not measure. It is unidentified rather than zero, which is a
   weaker statement than either, and I quote no value for it anywhere.

4. The dataset my proposal names, NELA-GT-2021, was withdrawn by its authors in
   January 2024. I verified this against the Harvard Dataverse API with a valid
   account. I have substituted SemEval-2019 Task 4, which is openly licensed and
   labels the article rather than the publisher.

One further development I would like your view on. A commercial lab has
published an audit of the same released checkpoint, in the same average-subject
configuration my instrument uses, reporting that its predictions are
anti-correlated with real cortex across four subjects. It is not peer reviewed,
it comes from a company selling the alternative, and I have not replicated it.
If it holds, it would explain why the coupling in point 3 has no predictive
value out of sample, and I think replicating it is the strongest piece of work
available to me after submission.

Section 3 of the attachment sets out what the thesis delivers with these changes
applied, and what it will not claim. Section 4 lists the decisions I need, three
of which block the write-up.

The apparatus is built and tested, and the 400-item corpus is complete,
audited, and analysed. The categories do separate, at an effect size the design
was powered to detect, though the separation is carried almost entirely by one
category and each category comes from a single source dataset, so I cannot
attribute it to framing rather than provenance. Section 3 states that
confound before the result. A second scanning session over 375 of the items
lets me report test-retest reliability: the separation replicates in that
session, and single-item scores are too noisy to call an individual item, so
the thesis makes category-level claims only and gives no per-item verdict. I will send a dated plan through to submission
separately this week, including the two journal articles.
